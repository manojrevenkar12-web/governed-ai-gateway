# Hiring, onboarding and growing the team

## The team we need

A team that works autonomously across three areas, with clear owners:

- **Platform engineering**: the gateway, policy engine, data protection, developer experience of the APIs.
- **Infrastructure**: deployment, scaling, PostgreSQL and Redis operations, observability, on-call tooling.
- **AI**: model and provider evaluation, agentic workflow tooling, prompt and evaluation practice for other teams.

Tech leads own architecture in their area. I own that the team delivers, grows and is trusted by its users.

## Hiring

Structured and the same for every candidate, so decisions compare people on evidence, not impressions.

1. **Recruiter screen** (30 min): motivation, logistics, salary range shared openly.
2. **Technical conversation** (60 min, two engineers): a system the candidate built that others depended on; what broke, how they knew.
3. **Work sample** (90 min, paired, no take-home): extend a small gateway feature (for example a per-team model allow-list) with tests. We look at reasoning, tests and communication, not completion.
4. **Design** (60 min): design a capability from the roadmap, with security and failure modes.
5. **Values and collaboration** (45 min, with a partner team): working with internal customers who disagree.

Each interviewer scores against a written rubric before the debrief, and the debrief starts from evidence.
Every interviewer is trained before joining the loop and shadows twice.

## Onboarding

- **Week 1**: laptop to first merged change; read the charter, ADRs and runbooks; shadow on-call.
- **Day 30**: owns a small capability end to end; has met the main internal customers.
- **Day 60**: on the on-call rotation as secondary; leads a design review.
- **Day 90**: development plan agreed; first feedback round in both directions.

## 1:1s and development

- Weekly 30-minute 1:1s, run by the engineer's agenda; career conversations once a quarter.
- Each engineer has a written development plan with two or three goals tied to real work, reviewed quarterly.
- Feedback is given when it is useful, not saved for reviews: specific, about the work, with an example.

## Performance

- Expectations by level are written down, so ratings compare against the same bar.
- Strong performance is recognised in public and reflected in scope and ratings.
- Low performance is addressed early and privately: the gap, the evidence, what good looks like, support offered,
  and a check-in date. Most gaps close here. If they do not, a time-boxed plan with HR follows.

## Keeping pace without burning out

- Protected learning time: two hours a week for the field (papers, new models, tools), shared in a monthly session.
- On-call load capped and reviewed; incident follow-ups count as planned work.
- Focus time: no meetings two afternoons a week.
